💧 Step 1: 闭式需求层 (Deterministic Demand)

首先计算在回收系统作用下的每日净补给需求。
工程变量: d_day_ton, D_year_ton
LaTeX 公式:
\begin{equation}
d_{\text{day}} = \frac{P \cdot \bar{w} \cdot (1 - \eta)}{1000} \quad (\text{tons/day})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D_{\text{year}} = 365 \cdot d_{\text{day}}
\end{equation}
1.2 物理可行性判据 (Sanity Check)
检查电梯单通道是否足以支撑生命线。

\begin{equation}
\text{Feasibility Check:} \quad d_{\text{day}} \le \frac{C_E}{365}
\end{equation}
Note: If this condition is violated, the colony is unsustainable without rockets or ISRU.

📦 Step 3: 库存动力学与策略 (Inventory Dynamics)
3.1 状态方程
定义每日库存更新逻辑。
工程变量: water_stock_ton[t], ship_elevator_ton[t], ship_rocket_ton[t]
LaTeX 公式:
\begin{equation}
W_{t+1} = W_t + x_{E,t} + x_{R,t} - d_{\text{day}}, \quad \forall t \in {0, \dots, 364}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{s.t.} \quad W_t \ge 0, \quad 0 \le x_{E,t} \le c_{E,\text{day}}^{(w)}, \quad 0 \le x_{R,t} \le c_{R,\text{day}}^{(w)}
\end{equation}
3.2 补给策略 (Order-up-to Policy)
采用 $(s, S)$ 策略确定每日计划运量。
工程变量: L_safe_days, target_stock_S
LaTeX 公式:
安全库存与目标库存：
\begin{equation}
s = L \cdot d_{\text{day}}, \quad S = (L + B) \cdot d_{\text{day}}
\end{equation}
补给决策（优先使用廉价电梯）：
\begin{equation}
x_t^{\text{plan}} = \min \left( c_{E,\text{day}}^{(w)} + c_{R,\text{day}}^{(w)}, \ \max(0, S - W_t) \right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
x_{E,t} = \min(x_t^{\text{plan}}, c_{E,\text{day}}^{(w)}), \quad x_{R,t} = x_t^{\text{plan}} - x_{E,t}
\end{equation}

💰 Step 4: 额外成本与时间线 (Cost & Timeline)
4.1 成本计算 (严格遵循两段式)
计算为了维持 $W_t \ge 0$ 所产生的总运输成本。
工程变量: total_cost_usd, cost_elevator, cost_rocket
LaTeX 公式:
总成本：
\begin{equation}
\Delta C = \Delta C_E + \Delta C_R
\end{equation}
电梯体系（包含 Apex 转运段）：
\begin{equation}
\Delta C_E = \sum_{t} \left( x_{E,t} \cdot c_E + \left\lceil \frac{x_{E,t}}{q_A} \right\rceil \cdot C_A \right)
\end{equation}
传统火箭体系（包含期望重发成本）：
\begin{equation}
\Delta C_R = \sum_{t} \left\lceil \frac{x_{R,t}}{q} \right\rceil \cdot \frac{C_L}{s_R}
\end{equation}

* 若 min(W_t) < 0: 输出 first_stockout_day。

\subsection{Model Extension: Lunar Ice ISRU (In-Situ Resource Utilization)}


Our baseline Q3 analysis assumes the Moon Colony relies entirely on Earth-based logistics to import the net water demand 
$d_{\text{day}}$ (tons/day), after accounting for recycling efficiency $\eta$.
While this ``full-import'' assumption is consistent with the problem requirement to use the delivery model, it also reveals a 
significant \emph{reliability premium}: under severe disruptions, maintaining a high service level requires expensive rocket backup, 
leading to a pronounced cost--mass asymmetry.


To evaluate the potential role of lunar ice mining (ISRU) without restructuring the logistics engine, we introduce a single additional
supply variable:
\begin{equation}
    u \;\; (\text{tons/day}) \qquad \text{the average local water production rate from lunar ice.}
\end{equation}
Define the ISRU coverage ratio as $\gamma = u / d_{\text{day}} \in [0,1]$.
Then the required \emph{Earth-shipped} net demand becomes the demand gap:
\begin{equation}
    d_{\text{day}}^{\text{ship}} = \max\{0,\; d_{\text{day}} - u\}
    \;=\; (1-\gamma)\, d_{\text{day}}.
\end{equation}


Importantly, the inventory dynamics and the stochastic disruption engines remain unchanged; only the demand term is replaced by
$d_{\text{day}}^{\text{ship}}$:
\begin{equation}
    W_{t+1} = W_t + x_{E,t}^{\text{actual}} + x_{R,t}^{\text{actual}} - d_{\text{day}}^{\text{ship}},
    \qquad t = 0,\dots,364,
\end{equation}
where $W_t$ is the on-Moon water inventory (tons), and $x_{E,t}^{\text{actual}}, x_{R,t}^{\text{actual}}$ are the realized delivered water
via the elevator chain and direct rockets, respectively.


This extension yields two actionable threshold insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Feasibility threshold.} If $u \ge d_{\text{day}}$, then $d_{\text{day}}^{\text{ship}}=0$ and Earth-based transportation is only
    needed for contingency buffers, dramatically reducing both cost and stockout risk.
    \item \textbf{Monotone risk--cost reduction.} For $0<u<d_{\text{day}}$, the annual logistics cost and stockout probability
    decrease monotonically with $\gamma$, quantifying the marginal value of investing in ISRU.
\end{itemize}


We therefore report a sensitivity sweep over $\gamma$ and compute the corresponding 
\emph{stockout probability} $P_{\text{stockout}}=\Pr(\min_t W_t < 0)$ and \emph{budget-at-risk} $\mathrm{VaR}_{0.95}(C)$,
under the same disruption presets used in the baseline Monte Carlo simulations.
These curves directly support the strategic recommendation that ISRU development is not merely optional but economically justified 
whenever its marginal cost per ton is below the implied transportation premium.
